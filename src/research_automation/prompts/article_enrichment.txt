Rules:
- Use only the supplied metadata, snippet, and extracted article content.
- Do not invent facts or details that are not present.
- If the information is unclear, say so plainly.
- Keep the summary concise.
- Extract approximately 4-8 concise factual points when the evidence supports them. Return fewer, including an empty list, when evidence is limited.
- Prioritize important numbers and units; dates, deadlines, and milestones; named companies, governments, and projects; investments and capacities; policy changes; and confirmed operational or commercial implications.
- Clearly preserve whether a development is proposed, under negotiation, announced, approved, under construction, completed, or operational.
- Every factual point must be directly supported by the supplied article content or snippet. Never invent or infer facts merely to reach a target count.
- Relevance score must be an integer from 1 to 5.
- Editorial significance must be an integer from 1 to 5.
- Keep relevance and editorial significance separate. Relevance measures how closely the article serves SOMA MATER's mission. Editorial significance measures how important the reported development itself is.
- Assess editorial significance from supported scale and measurable impact; concrete investment, capacity, or policy changes; strategic or commercial consequences; and whether the development is genuinely new or consequential.
- Use the extracted article content when available. When it is unavailable, score significance conservatively from the title and snippet.
- Do not reward speculative implications, promotional language, or impressive-sounding wording. A highly significant off-mandate development must still receive a low relevance score.
- The significance reason must be one concise sentence grounded in the supplied evidence.
- Topic and region labels should stay compatible with Notion where possible.
- Score relevance narrowly. SOMA MATER's primary mission is Food & Water Security and the Net Zero Transition, especially their strategic, policy, commercial, investment, infrastructure, and operational implications in MENA.
- Direct mission evidence includes food systems, agriculture, crops, fertilizer, water security, water treatment and reuse, desalination, decarbonization, renewable energy, solar, wind, batteries, storage, grids, electrification, and closely related themes.
- Oil, gas, ports, shipping, trade, regulation, regional conflict, or broad economic importance are contextual signals only. They do not establish high relevance unless the supplied facts show a concrete consequence for a direct mission area.
- Legitimate indirect relevance is allowed. For example, a shipping disruption that materially affects fertilizer availability and therefore food security can be highly relevant when that chain is explicit in the supplied article.
- Do not score an article highly just because it is from the Middle East, from a preferred source, or about business in general.
- General politics, consumer technology, retail, lifestyle, celebrity, or broad macro stories with no clear link to the focus areas should score 1 or 2.
- The geographic scope is strict: {{ geographic_scope }}
- Local stories outside that scope should score 1 or 2 even when they mention water, energy, climate, infrastructure, or trade. Examples: a city water project in India, a US local infrastructure grant, or a Europe-only climate event.
- The "why_it_matters" field must name the mission connection used to justify the score. If that connection cannot be explained from the supplied facts, the score cannot exceed 3.

Relevance guide:
- 5 = directly and centrally addresses a core mission area in MENA, or a genuinely global system with an explicit connection to MENA
- 4 = a clear and explicitly supportable connection to a direct mission area; the connection must be stated in "why_it_matters"
- 3 = legitimately adjacent or indirect, but the mission consequence is limited, uncertain, or not central to the story
- 2 = weak relevance driven mostly by regional, geopolitical, conventional-oil, shipping, trade, or broad economic context
- 1 = off-mandate for the newsletter

Editorial significance guide:
- 5 = major development with substantial, clearly supported implications
- 4 = important, concrete development with meaningful implications
- 3 = useful but relatively limited development
- 2 = minor, incremental, or weakly substantiated development
- 1 = little material significance
