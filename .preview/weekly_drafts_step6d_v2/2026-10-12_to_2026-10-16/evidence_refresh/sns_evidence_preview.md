Topic 1: Sharjah’s Infrastructure Boost for Water, Ports, and Waste-to-Energy

Hook: Sharjah is gearing up for its next growth cycle with a major water desalination project, extended port operations, and a doubling of waste-to-energy capacity.

Define the Issue: Meeting rising demand for water, logistics, and clean energy infrastructure is critical for Sharjah’s economic expansion and urban population growth.

Describe the Situation: By 2028, Sharjah’s first Independent Water Project will deliver 410,000 cubic metres of desalinated water daily using seawater reverse osmosis, enough for about 1.4 million people. In 2023, Sharjah Ports Authority extended its concession with Gulftainer for 35 more years, securing long-term port operations. Additionally, the Al Dhaid Multi-Modal Trade Corridor is planned to integrate Khorfakkan Port with Etihad Rail, targeting 1.5 million TEU annual capacity. Sharjah’s waste-to-energy facility, initially financed with $220 million and operated by BEEAH and Tadweer Group, is expanding its waste processing from 300,000 to 600,000 tonnes annually and doubling power generation capacity from 30 MW to 60 MW.

So Why Does This Matter? Sharjah’s scale-up in desalination addresses critical water security amid regional scarcity. The port extension and trade corridor aim to strengthen logistics and supply chain resilience. Meanwhile, doubling waste-to-energy capacity reflects a strategic pivot to circular economy solutions and local power generation, reducing reliance on fossil fuels.

Call-to-Action: For stakeholders in MENA infrastructure and sustainability, the question is how Sharjah’s integrated approach to water, waste, and logistics can serve as a model for balancing urban growth with resource constraints.

Topic 2: Egypt’s New Lithium-Ion Battery Assembly Facility Targets Energy Security

Hook: Egypt is partnering with Russia’s Rosatom to build a 500 MWh annual lithium-ion battery assembly plant, aiming to localize energy storage production.

Define the Issue: As Egypt’s renewable energy share grows, reliable energy storage and supply chain autonomy become essential for grid stability and industrial electrification.

Describe the Situation: Teletech and Rosatom signed an agreement to establish a lithium-ion battery assembly facility in Egypt with a 500 MWh per year production capacity. The facility will serve sectors including power generation, telecommunications, data centers, oil and gas, and other industrial applications. This project aims to reduce Egypt’s dependence on imported battery technologies and build local technical expertise to support the country’s energy transition.

So Why Does This Matter? Developing domestic battery assembly capacity is a strategic move to secure supply chains amid global competition and geopolitical risks. It also supports Egypt’s ambitions to integrate more renewables by enhancing grid flexibility and industrial energy resilience.

Call-to-Action: Energy and industrial planners should assess how localized battery manufacturing can mitigate supply disruptions and accelerate Egypt’s clean energy deployment.
