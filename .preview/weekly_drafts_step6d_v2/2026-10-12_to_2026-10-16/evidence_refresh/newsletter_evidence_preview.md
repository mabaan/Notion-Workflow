## Sharjah’s Infrastructure Leap: Water, Ports, and Waste-to-Energy Scale Up  
#WaterSecurity #EnergyTransition

Sharjah is advancing a comprehensive infrastructure upgrade that includes its first Independent Water Project, set to deliver 410,000 cubic metres of desalinated water daily by 2028. This project, using seawater reverse osmosis technology, aims to meet the needs of roughly 1.4 million residents. Complementing this, the Sharjah Ports Authority extended its port concessions with Gulftainer for an additional 35 years in 2023, securing long-term logistics capacity. The Al Dhaid Multi-Modal Trade Corridor, a planned public-private partnership, will integrate Khorfakkan Port with Etihad Rail, targeting an annual throughput of 1.5 million TEUs.

The waste-to-energy sector is also doubling its scale, expanding processing capacity from 300,000 to 600,000 tonnes annually and increasing power generation from 30 MW to 60 MW. This facility, initially financed with $220 million and operated jointly by BEEAH and Tadweer Group, exemplifies Sharjah’s commitment to circular economy principles and energy recovery. Together, these investments reflect a coordinated strategy to enhance water security, waste management, and logistics infrastructure, critical for sustaining Sharjah’s economic resilience and growth trajectory.

These developments align with broader UAE and MENA priorities to diversify infrastructure assets while managing resource constraints and environmental impact. Sharjah’s model highlights the role of public-private partnerships in extending operational horizons and capacity. The integration of port logistics with rail and water infrastructure also positions the emirate to better compete in regional trade flows and industrial expansion, reinforcing its strategic economic role.

SOMA’s Perspective: Sharjah’s multi-pronged infrastructure expansion illustrates a balancing act between scaling capacity and embedding sustainability in utility services. For external investors and policymakers, the key question is how these projects will integrate evolving regulatory frameworks on water use, emissions, and waste processing standards. Monitoring the operationalization of the Al Dhaid corridor and its rail connectivity will also be critical to assess Sharjah’s logistics competitiveness in a shifting regional trade landscape.

## Egypt’s New Battery Assembly Plant: Localizing Energy Storage Capacity  
#EnergyTransition #SupplyChains

Egypt is set to establish a lithium-ion battery assembly facility through a partnership between local company Teletech and Russia’s Rosatom energy storage unit. The plant will have an annual production capacity of 500 MWh, targeting sectors including power generation, telecommunications, data centers, oil and gas, and other industrial applications. This initiative aims to reduce Egypt’s reliance on imported battery technologies and external supply chains while building domestic technical expertise.

The facility’s capacity aligns with Egypt’s growing renewable energy share, supporting grid stability amid increasing variable generation. By localizing battery assembly, Egypt could improve supply chain resilience and shorten lead times for critical energy storage components. This move also signals a strategic step toward developing a more self-sufficient energy technology manufacturing base, which is essential for the country’s next phase of energy transition and industrial diversification.

Regionally, Egypt’s battery plant could become a hub for energy storage solutions in North Africa, given its strategic location and market size. The project reflects a broader trend in MENA to invest in energy storage as a key enabler for renewable integration and grid modernization. However, scaling up manufacturing and maintaining cost competitiveness will be ongoing challenges as global battery markets evolve.

SOMA’s Perspective: Egypt’s battery assembly plant represents a pivotal move to localize a critical segment of the energy transition supply chain. Stakeholders should watch how this facility integrates with domestic renewable projects and whether it can catalyze downstream manufacturing or innovation clusters. The trade-off between building local capacity and competing with established global suppliers will shape the plant’s commercial viability and regional influence.

## Iraq’s Solar Ambitions: Negotiations with Saudi Acwa Signal Regional Energy Ties  
#EnergyTransition #RenewableEnergy

Iraq is currently in negotiations with Saudi Arabia’s Acwa company to expand its solar energy capacity. While details remain limited, this dialogue reflects Iraq’s intent to accelerate renewable energy development as part of its broader energy transition strategy. Acwa, a major player in the MENA renewable sector, brings experience and capital that could help Iraq scale its solar infrastructure.

This potential partnership has implications beyond project financing; it could foster deeper regional collaboration on clean energy deployment. For Iraq, expanding solar capacity addresses urgent needs to diversify its energy mix, reduce fossil fuel dependence, and improve electricity supply reliability. For Saudi Arabia and Acwa, engaging in Iraq’s market offers a strategic foothold in a neighboring country with significant untapped renewable potential.

The talks occur amid a complex regional energy landscape where cross-border cooperation is increasingly important to meet climate goals and energy security. Iraq’s solar ambitions, if realized through partnerships like with Acwa, could contribute to shifting MENA’s energy profile and stimulate related infrastructure and industrial opportunities.

SOMA’s Perspective: Iraq’s negotiations with Acwa highlight a key regional dynamic where energy transition is intertwined with geopolitical and economic considerations. External observers should assess how regulatory frameworks, security conditions, and financing structures evolve to enable or constrain such cross-border renewable projects. The balance between local capacity building and foreign partnership will be critical to Iraq’s solar sector trajectory.
