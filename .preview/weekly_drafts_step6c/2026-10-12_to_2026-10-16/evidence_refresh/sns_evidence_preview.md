Topic 1: Sharjah’s Infrastructure Boost for Water, Waste, and Ports

Hook: Sharjah is gearing up for long-term growth with major infrastructure investments in water, waste, and port operations.

Define the Issue: Reliable water supply, efficient waste management, and robust port logistics are critical for sustaining urban growth and economic diversification in the UAE.

Describe the Situation: By 2028, Sharjah will commission its first Independent Water Project producing 410,000 cubic metres of desalinated water daily using seawater reverse osmosis, developed by SEWA and ACWA Power. The Sharjah Ports Authority secured a 35-year extension with Gulftainer to operate Sharjah Container Terminal and Khorfakkan Container Terminal, ensuring operational stability through 2058. Complementing this, the Al Dhaid Multi-Modal Trade Corridor is planned as a public-private partnership to handle 1.5 million TEUs annually, connecting Khorfakkan Port with Etihad Rail. On the energy and waste front, Sharjah’s waste-to-energy facility, operated by BEEAH and Tadweer Group, is doubling its annual waste processing capacity from 300,000 to 600,000 tonnes and increasing power generation from 30 MW to 60 MW, backed by a $220 million initial investment.

So Why Does This Matter? These projects collectively enhance Sharjah’s resilience in water security, reduce landfill dependence through waste-to-energy expansion, and extend port capacity critical for trade flows. The diversified financing models—from independent utility projects to long-term concessions and joint ventures—reflect a strategic approach to infrastructure investment that balances risk and operational control. For SOMA MATER’s focus areas, the desalination and waste-to-energy expansions directly support water security and net zero transition goals by increasing sustainable resource management and clean energy generation.

Call-to-Action: Monitor how Sharjah’s integrated infrastructure upgrades influence regional supply chains and environmental footprints, particularly the role of independent water projects and waste-to-energy scaling in MENA’s net zero pathways.

Topic 2: Egypt’s New Lithium-Ion Battery Assembly Facility

Hook: Egypt is advancing its energy storage capabilities with a new lithium-ion battery assembly plant targeting critical sectors.

Define the Issue: Expanding local energy storage manufacturing is essential for reducing import dependency and supporting the integration of renewables into power grids.

Describe the Situation: Teletech and Rosatom’s energy storage unit have signed an agreement to establish a lithium-ion battery assembly facility in Egypt with an annual production capacity of 500 MWh. The facility will serve power utilities, telecom networks, data centers, oil and gas, and industrial sectors. This initiative aims to build local technical expertise and enhance grid stability as Egypt increases its renewable energy share.

So Why Does This Matter? Developing domestic battery assembly capacity addresses supply chain vulnerabilities and supports Egypt’s energy transition by enabling more reliable energy storage solutions. For SOMA MATER’s strategic priorities, this project highlights a move toward localized manufacturing in the MENA region, which is crucial for scaling clean energy infrastructure and reducing reliance on external technology imports.

Call-to-Action: Track the facility’s impact on Egypt’s energy storage ecosystem and its potential to catalyze further regional investment in battery manufacturing and renewable integration.
