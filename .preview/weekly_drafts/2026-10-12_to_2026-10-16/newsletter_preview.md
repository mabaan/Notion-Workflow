## Sharjah’s Infrastructure Leap: Water, Waste, and Ports Ready for Growth  
#WaterSecurity #Infrastructure

Sharjah is advancing a major infrastructure upgrade that includes expanding daily water capacity by 410,000 cubic meters, extending port operations for another 35 years, and increasing waste handling capacity to 600,000 tonnes. These developments are designed to support the emirate’s next phase of economic growth and urban expansion.

Operationally, these investments will enhance Sharjah’s ability to meet rising demand for water, improve waste management efficiency, and sustain port activity critical to trade and logistics. Extending port operations also secures Sharjah’s role as a regional maritime hub, while the increased waste capacity addresses environmental management challenges tied to urbanization.

Within the UAE’s broader context, Sharjah’s focus on water and waste infrastructure reflects pressing regional priorities around resource scarcity and sustainable urban planning. As Gulf cities grow, balancing economic expansion with resilient infrastructure becomes essential to mitigate environmental risks and maintain competitiveness.

SOMA’s Perspective:  
Sharjah’s integrated approach to water, waste, and port infrastructure signals a strategic alignment of resource security with economic diversification. The scale of water capacity expansion points to anticipated population and industrial growth, while the port extension ensures continuity in trade flows amid regional competition. Investors and operators should watch how these upgrades influence Sharjah’s attractiveness as a logistics and industrial center, especially as water scarcity pressures mount across the Gulf.

## Egypt’s Battery Assembly Plant: A New Chapter in Energy Manufacturing  
#EnergyTransition #SupplyChains

Egypt is preparing to launch a lithium-ion battery assembly facility, signaling a significant step in its energy and manufacturing sectors. This development aligns with the country’s broader ambitions to participate more actively in clean energy technologies and industrial diversification.

The facility is expected to bolster Egypt’s capacity to produce critical components for renewable energy storage and electric mobility. By localizing battery assembly, Egypt can reduce dependence on imports, enhance supply chain resilience, and potentially position itself as a regional hub for battery technology manufacturing.

In the regional context, this move supports the MENA energy transition by strengthening the local production base for clean energy infrastructure. It also complements ongoing efforts across the Gulf and North Africa to develop integrated supply chains that can meet rising demand for batteries driven by solar, wind, and electric vehicle adoption.

SOMA’s Perspective:  
Egypt’s entry into lithium-ion battery assembly marks a strategic pivot toward capturing value in the clean energy supply chain rather than remaining solely an energy consumer. This facility could attract further investment and technology transfer, but the scale and integration with regional markets will determine its impact. For investors and policymakers, the key question is how Egypt leverages this capacity to build competitive advantages amid global battery supply chain shifts and regional energy transition priorities.

## Iraq and Saudi’s Acwa: Negotiating Solar Capacity Expansion  
#EnergyTransition #Investments

Iraq is currently in negotiations with Saudi Arabia’s Acwa Power to increase its solar energy capacity. These talks represent a collaborative effort between the two countries to develop renewable energy infrastructure and support Iraq’s energy diversification goals.

If successful, the partnership would bring Acwa’s expertise and investment into Iraq’s solar sector, potentially accelerating project development timelines and improving energy security. This cooperation could also open pathways for further cross-border investments and technology exchange in the renewable energy domain.

This development fits into a larger pattern of regional engagement on clean energy, where Gulf-based companies play a pivotal role in financing and operating renewable projects beyond their borders. It reflects the growing recognition that energy transition in the Middle East requires coordinated efforts and shared resources.

SOMA’s Perspective:  
The Iraq–Acwa talks underscore the strategic role of Gulf investors in shaping the renewable energy landscape across the region. For Iraq, attracting established players like Acwa is critical to overcoming infrastructure and investment gaps. However, the success of such partnerships depends on regulatory clarity, project bankability, and geopolitical stability. For regional stakeholders, these negotiations highlight the balancing act between national energy ambitions and the need for integrated, scalable renewable solutions.
