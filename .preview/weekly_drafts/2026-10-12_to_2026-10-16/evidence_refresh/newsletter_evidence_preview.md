## Sharjah’s Infrastructure Boost: Water, Waste, and Ports for the Next 35 Years  
#WaterSecurity #Infrastructure

Sharjah is advancing a multi-pronged infrastructure upgrade to underpin its future growth. By 2028, the emirate will commission its first Independent Water Project, producing 410,000 cubic meters of desalinated water daily using seawater reverse osmosis technology. This facility aims to serve approximately 1.4 million residents. In parallel, Sharjah Ports Authority extended its concession with Gulftainer for an additional 35 years in 2023, securing long-term operational continuity. The Al Dhaid Multi-Modal Trade Corridor, a planned public-private partnership, will integrate Khorfakkan Port with Etihad Rail, targeting an annual throughput of 1.5 million TEUs. Sharjah is also expanding its waste-to-energy capacity from 300,000 to 600,000 tonnes annually, doubling power generation from 30 MW to 60 MW through a $220 million project operated by BEEAH and Tadweer Group.

These investments directly enhance Sharjah’s operational capabilities in critical sectors. The desalination project addresses growing water demand and supports urban and industrial needs. The port concession extension and trade corridor development will boost logistics capacity and connectivity, reinforcing Sharjah’s role as a regional trade hub. Meanwhile, expanding waste-to-energy capacity aligns with sustainability goals by converting waste into power, reducing landfill reliance, and contributing to the emirate’s net zero transition.

Regionally, Sharjah’s integrated approach reflects broader Gulf priorities to secure water resources, optimize waste management, and modernize transport infrastructure amid rapid urbanization and economic diversification. The synergy between these projects positions Sharjah to better manage resource constraints and support resilient growth within the UAE’s competitive landscape.

SOMA’s Perspective: Sharjah’s coordinated investments illustrate a strategic balancing act between immediate resource demands and long-term sustainability. The desalination capacity is a critical hedge against water scarcity risks, while the extended port concession ensures stable trade flows amid shifting global supply chains. The waste-to-energy expansion not only addresses environmental pressures but also creates a new power source, reducing reliance on fossil fuels. These moves collectively enhance Sharjah’s economic resilience but require careful integration of infrastructure timelines and financing to realize their full potential.

## Egypt’s Leap into Battery Manufacturing: Localizing Energy Storage Capacity  
#EnergyTransition #Manufacturing

Egypt is set to establish a lithium-ion battery assembly facility through a partnership between local firm Teletech and Russia’s Rosatom energy storage unit. The plant will have an annual production capacity of 500 MWh, targeting sectors such as power generation, telecommunications, data centers, oil and gas, and industrial applications. This initiative aims to reduce Egypt’s dependence on imported battery technologies and external supply chains, while building local technical expertise in energy storage.

Operationally, the facility will enhance Egypt’s ability to support grid stability as renewable energy penetration increases. By producing batteries domestically, the country can better manage supply chain risks and costs associated with importing critical components. The project also aligns with broader industrial development goals by fostering local manufacturing capabilities in a high-growth technology segment.

In the regional context, Egypt’s move reflects the growing importance of energy storage in MENA’s energy transition. As countries expand renewable capacity, reliable storage solutions become essential to balance intermittent supply and demand. Egypt’s facility could serve as a model for other nations seeking to strengthen their energy technology ecosystems and reduce vulnerability to global market fluctuations.

SOMA’s Perspective: This battery assembly plant marks a significant step in Egypt’s strategic push to localize key clean energy technologies. The 500 MWh capacity is substantial for regional standards and positions Egypt to capture emerging market opportunities in energy storage. However, scaling production and integrating with renewable projects will require sustained investment and workforce development. The partnership with Rosatom also signals geopolitical dimensions in technology collaboration, which could influence supply chain security and export potential.

## Iraq Eyes Solar Expansion with Saudi Arabia’s Acwa: Negotiations Underway  
#EnergyTransition #Investments

Iraq is currently in negotiations with Saudi Arabia’s Acwa company to increase its solar energy capacity. While details remain limited, the talks indicate a potential partnership aimed at scaling up Iraq’s renewable energy infrastructure. This development aligns with Iraq’s broader energy transition objectives and efforts to diversify its power generation mix.

If successful, the deal could bring operational and investment benefits by leveraging Acwa’s experience in large-scale solar projects across the region. It may also unlock new financing avenues and technical expertise necessary for Iraq to expand clean energy capacity amid ongoing energy demand growth and environmental challenges.

Regionally, this negotiation highlights increasing cross-border collaboration in renewable energy development within the Gulf and MENA. Iraq’s engagement with a Saudi firm reflects shifting dynamics where regional players seek to pool resources and knowledge to accelerate net zero transitions, despite differing market maturity and political contexts.

SOMA’s Perspective: The talks between Iraq and Acwa underscore the strategic importance of regional partnerships in overcoming infrastructure and investment barriers in renewables. Acwa’s track record could help Iraq fast-track solar deployment, but success depends on navigating Iraq’s complex regulatory and security environment. This initiative also raises questions about how Gulf capital and expertise will influence Iraq’s energy market evolution and whether it can catalyze broader regional integration in clean energy supply chains.
