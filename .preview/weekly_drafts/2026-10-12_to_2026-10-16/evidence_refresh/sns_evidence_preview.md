Topic 1: Sharjah’s Infrastructure Expansion Targets Water, Ports, and Waste Management

Hook: Sharjah is gearing up for its next growth phase with major infrastructure projects across water, ports, and waste-to-energy sectors.

Define the Issue: Meeting rising demand for water, efficient port operations, and sustainable waste management are critical for Sharjah’s economic resilience and urban growth.

Describe the Situation: By 2028, Sharjah will commission its first Independent Water Project, producing 410,000 cubic metres of desalinated water daily using seawater reverse osmosis technology. This will serve about 1.4 million residents, addressing water security amid regional scarcity. The Sharjah Ports Authority extended its concession with Gulftainer for 35 years, ensuring continuity in port operations. Plans for the Al Dhaid Multi-Modal Trade Corridor will integrate Khorfakkan Port with Etihad Rail, targeting an annual throughput of 1.5 million TEUs. On waste management, Sharjah is doubling its waste-to-energy capacity from 300,000 to 600,000 tonnes per year and increasing power generation from 30 MW to 60 MW, with a $220 million investment led by BEEAH and Tadweer Group.

So Why Does This Matter? These projects reduce Sharjah’s dependence on external water sources and fossil fuels, enhance trade logistics, and convert waste into clean energy. The integrated approach supports urban sustainability and economic diversification while mitigating resource risks.

Call-to-Action: For SOMA MATER, Sharjah’s model highlights the importance of combining water security with circular waste solutions and resilient port infrastructure to underpin regional growth and net zero ambitions.

Topic 2: Egypt’s New Lithium-Ion Battery Facility Advances Energy Storage Capacity

Hook: Egypt is set to build a 500 MWh lithium-ion battery assembly plant, boosting local energy storage manufacturing.

Define the Issue: Expanding domestic energy storage capacity is key to integrating renewables and stabilizing power grids in emerging markets.

Describe the Situation: Teletech and Russia’s Rosatom signed an agreement to establish a lithium-ion battery assembly facility in Egypt with an annual capacity of 500 MWh. The plant will serve sectors including power generation, telecommunications, data centers, oil and gas, and industrial applications. This partnership aims to reduce Egypt’s reliance on imported battery technologies and external supply chains while building local technical expertise.

So Why Does This Matter? As Egypt increases its renewable energy share, reliable storage solutions become essential to grid stability and energy security. Developing local manufacturing capabilities also mitigates supply chain risks and supports industrial diversification.

Call-to-Action: SOMA MATER should monitor how regional energy storage projects like this reshape supply chains and technical capacity, informing strategies for resilient net zero transitions.
